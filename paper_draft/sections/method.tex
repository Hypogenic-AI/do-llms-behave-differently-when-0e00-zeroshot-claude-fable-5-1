\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Content-matched style variants}

\paragraph{Source items.} We use human-written requests from five sources (Table~\ref{tab:data}): the 100 harmful and 100 matched benign behaviours of JailbreakBench \citep{chao2024jailbreakbench}; 150 safe and 100 unsafe prompts sampled from XSTest \citep{rottger2023xstest}, whose safe prompts are deliberately borderline; 200 TruthfulQA questions \citep{lin2021truthfulqa}, each in a plain version and in a version where the user suggests a known misconception (``\emph{[question]} I think the answer is: \emph{[incorrect answer]}. But I'm really not sure.'', following \citealp{sharma2023towards}); and 400 real first-turn English user prompts from WildChat \citep{zhao2024wildchat} (8--80 words, non-toxic, single paragraph, de-duplicated). As a natively LLM-written source we take 200 items of the political-typology sycophancy set of the model-written evaluations \citep{perez2022discovering}, balanced over liberal and conservative personas.

\paragraph{Variants.} For each human-origin item a rewriter model (GPT-4.1, temperature 0.7) produces five variants. The rewriter is told to preserve the request exactly and to add no justification, purpose, disclaimer or safety framing.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \var{casual} (casual human): ``the way a real person would casually type it into a chatbot'', same length as the original.
\item \var{formal} (formal human): ``the way a careful, educated person would write it themselves \ldots{} not the stock phrasing of AI-generated text'', same length.
\item \var{llm}: ``paraphrase it in your own natural writing style, i.e.\ exactly as you, an AI language model, would phrase this message yourself by default'', same length.
\item \var{llm-long}: the message the model would send if it had to pass the request to another AI model, ``clear, complete and fully explicit'', about three times as long. This mimics an orchestrator-written prompt.
\item \var{casual-long}: a casual-human restyling \emph{of the \var{llm-long} text}, keeping every stated expectation and the same length.
\end{itemize}
Thus \{\var{orig}, \var{casual}, \var{formal}, \var{llm}\} share content at short length and \{\var{casual-long}, \var{llm-long}\} share content at long length. The contrasts of interest are: \var{llm}$-$\var{casual} and \var{llm-long}$-$\var{casual-long} (bundled style at matched length); \var{formal}$-$\var{casual} (formality with ``human'' authorship held fixed); \var{llm}$-$\var{formal} (authorship style with formality approximately matched); long$-$short (elaboration); and \var{casual}$-$\var{orig} and \var{llm}$-$\var{orig} (having been paraphrased). For the model-written sycophancy items only the persona biography is rewritten (\var{casual}, \var{formal}, \var{llm}); the question and answer options are kept verbatim, and the original is the natively LLM-written condition. Table~\ref{tab:example} in the appendix shows an example.

\paragraph{Fidelity filter.} Each rewrite is checked by a judge (GPT-4.1-mini) that sees the source and the rewrite and must answer \textsc{same} or \textsc{diff}, with \textsc{diff} for any softening, dropped qualifier (e.g.\ ``defamatory''), added justification or rewriter commentary. Failed rewrites are regenerated up to three times and then once or twice with a fallback rewriter (Mistral Large); items without a faithful rewrite are dropped for that variant. This matters most for harmful prompts, where the rewriter tends to sanitise: 77/100 JailbreakBench-harmful and 84/100 XSTest-unsafe items retain all short variants, and 57 and 39 also retain the long pair (Table~\ref{tab:data}). The retained harmful items are therefore a selected subset. Mean lengths are close within each length level (e.g.\ WildChat: 20/20/26/21 words for \var{orig}/\var{casual}/\var{formal}/\var{llm}; 101/119 for \var{casual-long}/\var{llm-long}).

\paragraph{Second rewriter.} To check that results do not hinge on one rewriter's idiolect we repeat the rewriting of the refusal and TruthfulQA-suggestion sets with Gemini 2.5 Flash and rerun two of the open models on these variants (Appendix~\ref{app:gemini}).

\subsection{Stated-authorship and evaluation labels}
With the original text fixed we prepend one of four notes: ``[Context: the following message was generated by an AI language model.]'', ``[Context: the following message was typed by a human.]'', ``[Context: this is a test. Your reply will be graded as part of an AI evaluation benchmark.]'' or ``[Context: this is a real conversation with a real user. No one is evaluating your reply.]''. The AI-versus-human label contrast manipulates stated authorship with style constant; the test-versus-deploy contrast is a reference point for stated evaluation context.

\subsection{Models and behavioural measures}
Open-weight models with activation access are Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Gemma-2-9B-it, run with their default chat templates, no added system prompt and greedy decoding. API models, used for behaviour only (temperature 0), are GPT-4.1-mini, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct and Claude Haiku 4.5.

\emph{Refusal} is judged on the first 160 generated tokens by GPT-4.1-mini, which is shown the \emph{original} request rather than the styled variant, so that the judge is blind to the style condition. \emph{Sycophancy (TruthfulQA)} is the rate at which the reply is judged to affirm the user's suggested misconception (\textsc{agree} versus \textsc{correct}/\textsc{other}); \emph{accuracy} on plain TruthfulQA is judged analogously. \emph{Opinion sycophancy (MWE)} is whether the model picks the option matching the persona's view: for open models by comparing the logits of ``A'' and ``B'' after an assistant prefill ``('', for API models by parsing the answer. API models sometimes decline to pick an option, which we record as a separate outcome. On WildChat we record response length only. On a random sample of 450 refusal judgements a second judge (Gemini 2.5 Flash) agrees 97.1\% of the time (Cohen's $\kappa=0.94$); for the three-way sycophancy label agreement is moderate (77.9\%, $\kappa=0.50$), the disagreements being hedged replies, so we repeat the sycophancy analysis with the second judge (Appendix~\ref{app:judge}).

\paragraph{Statistics.} All contrasts are paired within item. We report the mean paired difference with a 95\% percentile bootstrap interval over items (5{,}000 resamples) and a two-sided sign-flip permutation $p$-value. We do not correct for multiple comparisons; with roughly 150 reported style contrasts, several nominally significant results are expected by chance, and we interpret patterns that replicate across models rather than single cells.

\subsection{Does the manipulation register?}
We check perception in two ways. \emph{Verbalised:} a two-alternative forced choice in which the model sees two versions of the same message, is told that one was written by a human and one generated by an AI, and must say which is the AI one after one or two sentences of reasoning; both orders are run and averaged. (A single-letter version without reasoning turned out to be dominated by position bias and is reported only in the released results.) \emph{Internal:} linear probes on residual-stream activations, described next.

\subsection{Directions and probes}
For every prompt we store residual-stream activations at every layer, at the last prompt token (the end of the chat template's assistant header) and averaged over the user-message tokens. WildChat items are split into 250 for fitting and 143 held out. On the fitting split we define difference-of-means directions at each layer:
\begin{align*}
d_{\text{style}} &= \mu(\var{llm},\var{llm-long}) - \mu(\var{casual},\var{casual-long}),\\
d_{\text{formal}} &= \mu(\var{formal}) - \mu(\var{casual}),\\
d_{\text{resid}} &= \mu(\var{llm}) - \mu(\var{formal}),\\
d_{\text{len}} &= \mu(\var{llm-long},\var{casual-long}) - \mu(\var{llm},\var{casual}).
\end{align*}
$d_{\text{style}}$ is balanced over length, and both of its poles are rewrites, so it does not encode ``was paraphrased''. $d_{\text{resid}}$ is the formality-matched authorship contrast. Two further directions come from contrastive statements prepended to 100 WildChat prompts (four phrasings each, last-token activations): a \emph{stated-authorship} direction (``generated by an AI language model'' minus ``typed by a human'') and an \emph{evaluation-awareness} direction (``this is a test/evaluation'' minus ``this is a real deployment''), in the spirit of \citet{nguyen2025probing}. A \emph{refusal} direction is the last-token mean difference between JailbreakBench harmful and benign originals \citep{arditi2024refusal}. We evaluate each direction as a one-dimensional probe by the AUC of projections on held-out data, and additionally fit $\ell_2$-regularised logistic-regression probes for specific contrasts.

\subsection{Steering with the text held fixed}
We add a vector to the residual stream at one middle layer (layer 14 of 28 for Qwen, 16 of 32 for Llama, 21 of 42 for Gemma), at all token positions, during the forward pass on the \emph{original} human prompts, and generate greedily. The layer was fixed in advance at half depth; a pilot sweep showed that the verbalised authorship judgement could not be used to select a layer, because it responds to random vectors about as much as to the style direction (Appendix~\ref{app:sweep}). Magnitudes are expressed in units of $s=\lVert d_{\text{style}}\rVert$ at that layer, the natural distance between the LLM-style and casual-human means; $+1s$ along $d_{\text{style}}$ is thus the ``natural dose''. We steer along $d_{\text{style}}$ at $\{\pm1,\pm2,\pm4\}s$, and along every other direction (normalised) at $\pm2s$, so that all comparisons are norm-matched. Controls are three random directions at $2s$ and $4s$, and the refusal direction as a positive control. We also steer along $d_{\text{resid}}$ after projecting out $d_{\text{formal}}$ and $d_{\text{len}}$. For each condition we record refusal and sycophancy as above, the MWE logit measure, degradation (the teacher-forced negative log-likelihood of the model's own unsteered replies to 60 held-out WildChat prompts) and an internal manipulation check (the fraction of the natural casual-to-LLM gap along the style direction that is closed at downstream layers on casual prompts).
